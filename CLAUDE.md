# Gulf

A personal fishing almanac for the Hauraki Gulf. The product is one static page: tides, sun, moon, solunar periods, weather, and a map of wind, flow and chop.

## Commands

| Command | Result |
|---|---|
| `npm install` | Installs the build dependencies. Use the Node version in `.nvmrc`; the publish workflow uses it too. |
| `npm run build` | Writes `dist/index.html`, one self-contained file, and beside it the service worker `sw.js`, the web app manifest, two icons, and the data files: the depth grid, `depth.<hash>.png`, its contours, `contours.<hash>.json`, and the chart marks, `marks.<hash>.json`. |
| `npm test` | Checks tide predictions against the LINZ 2028 table for Auckland, one day of sun, moon and solunar values against recorded results, the rules that find windows, the wind colors and click times of the Week rows, the leave-by time, the route's path search, clock, comfort scoring and ferry lane crossings, the depth at a point and at a time, and the decoder of the depth grid's PNG. |
| `python3 tools/screenshot.py` | Renders the built page with a mocked forecast, at an iPad size or a phone size, on any of the three screens. Needs Python Playwright. Read the header of the file for options. |
| `python3 tools/depth.py [Hauraki_topobathy_DEM.tif]` | Writes `data/depth.png` and `data/depth.json`, the depth below chart datum, from the 20 m Hauraki Gulf grid, and prints a report on its datum and coverage. Without a path it downloads the grid, 223 MB. Needs numpy. |
| `python3 tools/contours.py` | Writes `data/contours.json`, the depth contours at 2, 5, 10, 20, 30 and 50 m, from `data/depth.png`. Needs numpy. Run it after `tools/depth.py`. |

The page scripts are plain globals, loaded in this order: `Astronomy`, `Neaps`, `DATA`, `Engine`, then the UI. The build inlines `astronomy-engine` and a bundle of `@neaps/tide-predictor`.

## Rules

1. The product stays static, with no server. Beside `index.html`, `dist/` holds only the service worker, the web app manifest, the two icons and the data files: the depth grid, its contours and the chart marks. The name of each data file holds a hash of its content, and the build removes copies under older names.
2. Show every time in Pacific/Auckland time, whatever the device time zone. Use `Engine.parts`, `Engine.midnight`, `Engine.addDays` and `Engine.fmtTime`. Do not use the local getters of `Date`.
3. Never invent data. A forecast that is missing shows as missing.
4. Use US English spelling. Proper names keep theirs: "Waitematā Harbour".
5. Write interface text plainly: name the actor, make one claim per sentence, use no idiom.
6. The map uses no third-party component. It is SVG drawn from `data/coast.json`.
7. Check both themes after any layout change, at the iPad sizes in `tools/screenshot.py` and on a 390 px wide screen.
8. Open a pull request when a task is complete.

## Design

The look follows Braun products of the 1960s. Tokens are in `src/style.css`.

- Chassis `#F5F4F0` on a workbench `#E7E6E1`. Hairlines `#D4D2CA`. Ink `#1A1A18`.
- Text is Helvetica Neue, then Inter. Every number is JetBrains Mono with tabular figures.
- Each section heading carries an index number and a hairline above it.
- Color carries meaning and nothing else:

| Color | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Green `#2E8B57` | Active or selected. Windows inside the owner's limits. Solunar periods and the solunar line. |
| Amber `#E59400` | Needles on gauges, and the map probe. |
| Vermilion `#D9381E` | Alerts. On the Now screen, wind over the owner's limit is an alert. Wind from 20 knots in the day display and the tables. "Now" in the day display, on the Week screen and in the Now screen's next six hours. |
| Yellow `#DCA400`, disc `#F5C400` | The sun, in the day display. |
| Slate `#525866` | The moon, in the day display. |
| Blue `#2A6FA8` | The tide, in the day display and in the Now screen's next six hours. |
| Comfort levels, `--wind-0` to `--wind-4` | The route's levels, on the map and in the route panel: Easy green, Fair yellow-green, Bumpy yellow, Rough amber, Very rough red. They reuse the first five stops of the wind ramp, so the dark theme has them too. Gray marks a leg with no forecast. The level's name is always written beside its color. |
| Wind ramp, `--wind-0` to `--wind-5` | Wind on the Week rows, by speed as a share of the owner's limit: green up to 0.60, yellow-green at 0.87, yellow at the limit, amber just over it, red at 1.33 and dark red from 1.80. Yellow ends at the owner's limit, and amber starts just over it. `Engine.windColor` blends the stops in a straight line in RGB. |

- The day display has a light ground by day, `#FBFAF7`, and a darker one at night, `#D8D6CE`. It is never black.
- A dark theme serves devices in dark mode. Change both themes together.
- Motion is short and mechanical: 160 to 240 ms, `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1)`.

## Data and accuracy

**Tides.** The page predicts Auckland from 43 harmonic constants (TICON-4, by way of the Neaps database) and adds the LINZ mean sea level, 1.94 m above chart datum. Five constants were left out because they made the fit to LINZ worse: M3, T3, R3, 3L2, 3N2. Every other spot applies the LINZ Secondary Ports Table to the Auckland prediction: a time difference for high water, one for low water, a range ratio and a mean sea level. Those values are in `data/spots.json`.

**Measured fit.** Against 146 highs and lows in the LINZ 2028 Auckland table: median time difference 2.3 min, nine in ten within 6.1 min, largest 9 min, heights within 0.10 m. `npm test` guards these figures. The secondary spots have not been checked against LINZ daily tables.

**Solunar.** A major period is two hours centered on the moon's upper or lower transit. A minor period is one hour centered on moonrise or moonset. The day rating, the activity index and the fish levels are rules of thumb defined in `engine.js`. They are not measurements.

**Weather.** The browser fetches Open-Meteo. Each spot gets a forecast and marine data. The map gets a grid of 83 water points: wind from the forecast service, current from the marine service. The page keeps the last loaded data in `localStorage` under keys that start with `gulf-almanac-`. A load runs when the page opens, once a minute if the data is over an hour old, when the page returns to the foreground, and when the owner presses Sync. After a failed load, automatic loads wait 10 minutes. Sync, a return to the page and a restored connection try at once.

**Windows.** `Engine.windows` finds them. A window is a stretch between first light and last light (civil dawn and dusk) when the sustained wind stays at or under the owner's limit for the owner's minimum time or longer. Each hourly forecast value stands for the half hour either side of its time. The wind is compared in whole knots, as the page shows it. A missing hour ends a window. Gusts and chop do not close a window. The owner sets the two limits under Limits, and the iPad stores them in `localStorage` under `gulf-almanac-limits`, with the boat speed and margin for the run home; the defaults are 15 kn and 3 h. `Engine.rankWindows` puts the longest first. `test/windows.test.mjs` covers these rules.

**Week.** The Week screen shows today and the next six days. Every row shares one range of local hours: from the hour before the earliest first light to the hour after the latest last light. From its top, each row draws the major periods with their rating lamps, the wind, the windows' brackets and the tide. The wind is one bar for each forecast hour, with a tick at the gust. The wind field runs from calm at its foot to twice the owner's limit at its top, in every row, so the dotted limit line sits at mid height. A bar's height and color use the speed in whole knots, as the window rule compares it, so a bar inside a window never rises over the limit line and is never amber or red. Bars before first light and after last light are paler. Hours with no forecast are drawn only between first light and last light. Beside each row, the label gives the day with its solunar rating lamps, the wind between first light and last light, and the high waters. A click or tap on a lane, inside a window or not, opens the Day screen with the time under the pointer pinned. `Engine.laneTime` gives that time, rounded to 5 minutes and kept inside the row's day. A click on a window button pins the window's start when no pointer was released on the lane just before it, as with a key press. `docs/boat-check.md` asks the owner to check VoiceOver on the iPad. With a mouse, a hairline and a time flag follow the pointer over the lanes. `docs/week-rows.md` holds the design. A best-window card lists the major period that overlaps the window most, and the high and low waters inside it.

**Flow.** The current model has cells of about 8 km. It does not resolve the channels. `loadFlowField` in `src/ui/map.js` is the only code that knows the source, so a better source can replace it there.

**Screen layout.** The wide layout applies from 1000 px wide and 700 px tall: `isWide` in `src/ui/core.js` and the media query at the end of `src/style.css` use the same test. In it, the frame fills the window and the page does not scroll. Below 700 px of height, as on an iPad mini in Safari, the screens stack and the page scrolls. In the stacked layout, the map frame fills the width of its column and keeps the map's shape up to 75% of the window's height. Past that height, the frame stops growing, and the map shows more water at the sides. The frame has `width: 100%` because without it Safari narrows the frame to keep the map's shape. Day puts the map on the left, the day on the right and the time bar along the bottom, and the day display takes the height that is left. A draw while the Day screen is hidden, as when the Week screen opens a day, measures nothing and uses default sizes; `fitChart` in `src/ui/day.js` draws the display again when the screen shows or its box changes size. With a route open, the class `compact` on `.dayview` gives the map about 62% of the width, hides the day display, and puts the route panel under the one-row day; the panel scrolls in its part. A map frame under 316 px tall and at least 300 px wide, as on the Day screen of an iPad mini in landscape, puts its layer buttons in a row along its foot, so they stay clear of the view buttons. A container query on `.mapframe` in `src/style.css` sets this. Now puts the map on the left and the rest on the right, where the list of places scrolls in its part. On narrow screens, Now stacks its parts with the map last.

**Offline.** The service worker stores the page, the Latin font files and the data files on the device, so the page opens without a connection. It serves the page from the network first and falls back to the stored copy when the network fails, returns an error, or takes over 4 seconds. A new build therefore shows as soon as the device is online, and each build installs a new worker, which removes the old copy of the page. Only the page and the data files go through the worker: any other file opened on its own, such as an icon, passes straight to the network. Fonts come from the stored copy first. The data files are the depth grid, its contours and the chart marks. `build.mjs` gives the worker their names, which hold a hash of each file's content. The worker stores them on install, serves them from the stored copy first, and on activate removes every stored data file the build does not list. The worker does not handle forecasts; the page keeps those in `localStorage`. Browsers run a service worker only on https or on `localhost`, so a page opened from a file, as by `tools/screenshot.py`, runs without one. To test the worker, serve `dist/` on `localhost`.

**Saved places.** The owner saves the probe's point as a numbered mark or as home, from the probe reading under the map. The iPad stores them in `localStorage` under `gulf-almanac-marks` and `gulf-almanac-home`, as latitude and longitude. A deleted mark's number goes to the next new mark. A place may sit on land in the simplified coastline, as a marina often does; there the page gives a position and no wind, flow or chop estimate. Once home is set, the map's third preset centers on it.

**Where to go.** Under the map, a list ranks the owner's marks and up to three suggestions, lowest chop first, at the map's time. A suggestion is one of the calmest chop cells within 10 nautical miles of the starting point, at least 3 nautical miles from the other suggestions and 1 from any mark. It makes no claim about fish. On the Day screen the starting point is home, or the selected tide spot until home is set. On the Now screen it is the iPad's position. A dashed ring on the map shows the reach.

**Map view.** The map's view is a center and a zoom, `view` in `src/ui/map.js`. At zoom 1 the whole map fits its frame, and the viewBox takes the frame's shape. Zoom stops at 8, where a pixel on the largest screen is about 20 m and the coastline, simplified to about 30 m, is still true to about a pixel and a half. Pins, labels, arrows and the probe keep one size on screen at any zoom. Arrows sit 22 map units apart, or 11 once 22 units would be 70 px or more on screen. The presets are Whole Gulf, Inner Gulf, and 10 nautical miles around the starting point of Where to go. The Day and Now screens share one map: `moveMap` in `src/ui/now.js` moves it to the screen that shows, and each screen keeps its own view.

**Now.** The Now screen asks the iPad for its position while the screen shows and the page is in the foreground. Without a position, or with one outside the map, the screen says so and measures from the selected spot. The tide and the solunar periods come from the nearest tide spot, which the Tide card names. The wind and chop come from the grid at the position. A position on land in the simplified coastline, as at a marina, uses the nearest water cell within 1.5 nautical miles, and the cards say how far away that cell is. The four cards show the present. The map shows now or up to three hours ahead, and the screen opens at now. The next six hours show one hourly slot each for wind and chop, with night, the solunar periods, the turns of the tide and last light behind them. Run home is described below.

**Run home.** `Engine.leaveBy` gives the leave-by time: last light, less the trip home, less the owner's margin, rounded down to the minute. The trip is the route over open water from the Now screen's starting point to home at comfortable speeds, with any water between the path's ends and the places themselves at the owner's boat speed; the map draws its path dotted. The trip is scored for leaving at the leave-by time, found again up to three times, and the earliest leave-by time stands. The owner sets the speed and the margin under Limits. Both start unset, and until both are set, and home too, the page shows no leave-by time. Once the leave-by time has passed, it shows as an alert. Run home also gives the path's distance, the trip time and the worst stretch leaving now. `test/runhome.test.mjs` covers the arithmetic.

**Route comfort.** `docs/route-comfort.md` holds the design, which is built in steps; its build order table records which are done. `Engine.findPath` searches the map's water raster with A*, over eight neighbors. A water cell next to land is closed, which keeps the path about 200 m off the shore. An end on land or on a closed cell starts from the nearest open cell joined to the Gulf. The path is then straightened. When no open water joins the two ends, there is no path, and no straight line stands in for one. `Engine.routeLegs` cuts the path into legs of at most 0.3 nautical miles. `Engine.scoreRoute` scores each leg at the time the boat reaches it: it reads the chop height and the wind's direction at the leg's middle, scores the leg, then adds the leg's time at its comfortable speed to the clock. A leg's score is the largest of three terms: slam, from waves met head-on, which grows with the square of speed; roll, from waves on the beam; and follow, a floor for a following sea. The level shows the leg at cruising speed, and the comfortable speed is the fastest speed that keeps slam at or under 0.30 m. A leg the forecast does not cover gets no score. `Engine.joinStretches` joins neighboring legs of one level. Every number these rules use is in `Engine.COMFORT`, because the owner's trip ratings will correct them. On the Day screen, each row of Where to go and the probe reading have a Route button. A is the starting point of Where to go: home, or the selected spot until home is set. The route is scored for the time on the bar, which then reads Leave time; that time is always on the day shown, so a route on another day pins the start of the bar, and Now returns to today. The day display collapses to one Week row of the selected day, with the route panel under it, and the map takes the freed width. The map draws the path in the level colors, with A and B at its ends, and its Route view fits the path. Under the map, a strip shows the route by distance: the level of each stretch, the cause under each stretch wider than 70 px, the comfortable speed as a line, and a tick every 2 nautical miles. The strip takes the place of Where to go until a tap on the map opens a reading. `Engine.laneCrossings` finds where the path crosses Auckland Transport's ferry routes, from `data/ferries.json`: the strip marks each crossing, and the route panel counts them and names each lane. The lanes carry no timetable. `route.js` keeps the last four paths, so a new leave time scores again without a new search. Each screen with the map has its own route, in `state.routes`. On the Now screen, A is the iPad's position and follows the boat, the route leaves now, and the route panel and strip take the place of Where to go. `test/route.test.mjs` covers the worked examples in the design, the search on made-up rasters, and the clock.

**Depth in the route.** With a safe depth and a boat speed set, and the depth grid loaded, `scoreTrip` in `src/ui/route.js` keeps the path to water deeper than the safe depth. `Engine.cellDepths` gives each cell of the water raster the shallowest chart depth of its two by two depth cells, and its nearest tide spot, once. `Engine.lowestTides` gives each spot's lowest tide from the leave time to the arrival at cruising speed along the open-water path, plus 30 minutes. `Engine.shoalGrid` closes the cells whose depth at that tide is under the safe depth, and `Engine.findPath` searches what is left. When the scored arrival, at comfortable speeds, plus 30 minutes falls after that window, the window grows to it and the path is found again, up to three times in all. An end in shallow water starts from the nearest cell deep enough, up to 0.5 nautical miles farther than it moves to reach open water, and the panel says how far that is. An end farther than that gives no path. With no path, the panel says so and names the least depth on the open-water path, from `Engine.leastDepth`. While the depths load or after they fail to load, or with no boat speed, a set safe depth does not shape the path, and the panel and the run home say so. The panel gives the least depth on the path and its time, and the strip draws the depth of each leg at its time, with the safe depth dashed. The run home, which uses the same trip, keeps the same rule, and the map draws its dotted line along that trip's path. `test/routedepth.test.mjs` has a path that goes round a made-up shoal at low tide and crosses it at high tide, and an end cut off from deep water past its reach.

**Depth.** `tools/depth.py` reduces K. Mackay's 2025 Hauraki Gulf grid, 20 m cells under CC BY 4.0, to `data/depth.png`: one cell per quarter map unit, about 93 m, over the map's bounds. Before the reduction, the tool raises every value at or above LAT by 0.4 m, ramped in over the 0.5 m below LAT, because the LINZ drying heights read higher than the grid. Each cell keeps the shallowest of the 20 m values inside it, so a reduced cell never hides a shoal. The grid's depths are below Lowest Astronomical Tide (LAT), and the file takes LAT as chart datum, the level the tide heights use: the page's tide model puts LAT 0.10 m above chart datum at Auckland, so the file errs by 0.1 m on the side of less water. A cell's value is red × 256 + green: 0 means no data, 1 means land more than 4 m above LAT, and any other value is the depth below chart datum in decimeters plus 100, so a drying height is a negative depth. `data/depth.json` holds the bounds, the cell size, the encoding, the datum and the source. The grid covers the whole map. Its record gives LAT for the water and NZVD2016 for the land without saying where the two meet; `docs/depth-map.md` records the check against the LINZ charts that found the drying flats on LAT, and the comparison with the LINZ soundings that `tools/soundings.py` makes. Not for navigation.

**Two depths.** Chart depth is the value the depth grid stores for a cell. Depth at a time is chart depth plus the tide height at the tide spot nearest the point, which `Engine.nearestSpot` finds. `Engine.depthAt` and `Engine.depthAtTime` give them; each takes the decoded grid as an argument, as `findPath` takes its raster. A point on the edge between cells takes the shallowest cell it touches. The page fetches the grid when it opens and decodes it in script, with `Engine.pngChunks`, `DecompressionStream` and `Engine.pngValues`. No canvas sits in the path, because a browser may add noise to the bytes a canvas gives back. The probe reading gives the depth at the map's time, with the chart depth, the tide and the tide spot under it, and the words "Not for navigation". Where to go gives the same depth for each place. A point with no value says "No depth here". A drying cell above the tide says it is dry, and a land cell that it is above high water. A page opened from a file cannot fetch the grid, so it says the depths did not load; `tools/screenshot.py` takes `URL` to open the page from a local server.

**Depth on the map.** The Depth button, beside Wind, Flow and Chop, shows the depth layers, and it starts on. `tools/contours.py` traces the contours at 2, 5, 10, 20, 30 and 50 m through the centers of the cells of `data/depth.png`, so the lines agree with the depths the page gives. It drops closed lines shorter than about 740 m and simplifies the rest to within about 45 m. The map draws the contours as blue hairlines with labels in meters; labels show from zoom 1.8, and twice as many from zoom 3.6. With Chop off, or with no forecast shown, three blue tints show chart depth under 5 m, 5 to 10 m and 10 to 20 m. With the chop tint shown, the depth tints give way to it, and the contours take a stronger blue. Once the owner sets a safe depth under Limits, from 0.5 to 5.0 m, the map hatches water whose depth at the map's time is under it. Each cell takes the tide at its nearest tide spot, found once. Until the safe depth is set, the map hatches nothing. The tints and the hatching are pictures of the grid, one pixel per cell, laid over the map: the hatching is a mask over a pattern of lines. A key under the scale gives the time of the hatching, the tints while they show, the hatching, the contour line, and the words "Not for navigation", which stay on the map while depths show.

**Chart marks.** `tools/chartmarks.py` takes rocks, wrecks, obstructions, buoys, beacons, lights, restricted areas, marine farms and foul ground from the LINZ chart data (the "Hydro" layers, CC BY 4.0, not updated since May 2024). It keeps the Harbour scale where the Harbour charts reach, the Approach scale elsewhere, and the Coastal scale outside both. It joins each light to a buoy or beacon within 30 m, and writes each object's name and facts in words, with the texts listed once. The Marks button shows them, and it starts on. Marks show from zoom 2, in ink: a dotted ring with a cross for a rock, a hull for a wreck, a dotted ring for an obstruction, a diamond for a buoy, a triangle for a beacon, a star for a light on its own and a square for a marine farm. The side of a lateral mark is written, not colored, because color carries other meanings. Areas show as dashed outlines at every zoom. The probe reading names the mark within 18 px of the probe and each area around it. While marks show, the key keeps the words "Not for navigation" on the map.

**Coastline.** `data/coast.json` is LINZ's 1:50,000 coastline, CC BY 4.0, simplified to about 30 m, with islands over about half a hectare. The page inlines it, which makes `index.html` about 700 KB. It replaced the GSHHG coastline, simplified to about 110 m, in the coastline pull request; `docs/depth-map.md` records what changed in routes and chop.

**Chop.** The page measures fetch on a raster of the coastline, takes wave height and period from the JONSWAP growth curves, then applies a wave and current factor. The rating steps are 0.15, 0.3, 0.5 and 0.8 m. The owner has not yet confirmed these against the boat.
