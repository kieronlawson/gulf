Your limits

A window is a time between first light and last light when the wind stays at or under your limit for your minimum time or longer. The Week screen ranks windows, and the Day screen marks them.

Tides

The page computes Auckland tides from 43 harmonic constants (TICON-4, by way of the Neaps tide database) and adds the LINZ mean sea level of 1.94 m, so heights are above chart datum. For the other spots it applies the time differences and range ratios in the Secondary Ports Table of the New Zealand Nautical Almanac 2026–27.

Checked against 146 high and low waters in the LINZ table for Auckland in 2028: half the times agree within 3 minutes, nine in ten within 7 minutes, the largest gap is 9 minutes, and the heights agree within about 0.1 m. Weather moves the real water level. LINZ publishes the official tables.

Sun, moon and solunar periods

Sun and moon positions come from Astronomy Engine. A major period lasts two hours, centered on the moon passing overhead or underfoot. A minor period lasts one hour, centered on moonrise or moonset.

The day rating is a rule of thumb, not a measurement. Four fifths of the score comes from the moon phase, highest at new and full moon. One fifth comes from periods that fall on sunrise or sunset.

The activity index adds a rise for each period and for sunrise and sunset, scaled by the moon phase. The fish rate each period by the highest value of that index inside the period.

Wind, flow and chop on the map

Wind and current come from Open-Meteo at grid points 0.1° apart. The current model has cells of about 8 km. It does not resolve the channels and is unreliable near land.

Chop is an estimate. The page measures the open water upwind of a point, takes wave height and period from the JONSWAP growth curves, and assumes the wind has blown long enough. It then raises the waves where the current runs against them and lowers them where it runs with them. Over shallow water, once the depths load, it holds the waves to six tenths of the depth at the time. Where the waves in open water would be over half the depth, they likely break: the map outlines that water with a red dashed line, and the route rates it Very rough. Swell is not included.

The depth at a point is its chart depth plus the tide at the nearest tide spot, at the map's time. The chart depth comes from a grid of cells about 93 m across. Each cell holds the shallowest water the 20 m source grid shows inside it, so a cell can read shallower than the water at the point itself. A negative chart depth is a drying height.

Not for navigation. Read the marine forecast before you go out.

Reading the Day screen

The day display's ground is darker at night. Green bands mark solunar periods, and the green fish above each band rate that period from one to four. The red needle marks now. With a forecast loaded, a wind lane shows wind and gusts in knots.

On the map, each lamp is a tide spot. Solid arrows show wind and point downwind. Line arrows show flow and point downstream. A longer arrow means more wind or more flow. Dashed line arrows lie within 4 km of land, where the 8 km current model is unreliable.

Chop is an estimate from wind, fetch and flow: smooth under 0.15 m, light from 0.15 m, moderate from 0.3 m, rough from 0.5 m, very rough from 0.8 m.

Route draws a path over open water from home to a place, or on the Now screen from your position, and rates each part of it from Easy to Very rough at the time the boat gets there, leaving at the time on the bar. The comfortable speed is the fastest speed, up to your boat speed and not under 8 kn, at which waves from ahead slam the hull no harder than on a Fair part of the route. The path keeps about 200 m off land. Once you set a safe depth and a boat speed, it also keeps to water deeper than the safe depth at the lowest tide of the trip, from the leave time to the arrival plus 30 minutes. It is a line for scoring, not a route to steer.

Reading the Now screen

The Now screen measures from the iPad's position, and from the selected spot while it has none. The tide and the bite come from the nearest tide spot. Wind over your limit shows in red.

In the next six hours, each slot holds one hour: an arrow that points downwind, the wind in knots, and a bar for the chop. The ground is darker at night, green bands mark solunar periods, and blue lines mark high and low water.

Run home gives the latest time to leave: last light, less the trip home, less your margin. The trip follows a path over open water from your position to home, drawn dotted on the map, at comfortable speeds for the sea leaving now. Route, on either screen, draws a path to a place and rates it from Easy to Very rough.
